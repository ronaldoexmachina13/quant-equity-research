Can simple factor strategies beat holding the whole universe?

A quantitative research project testing three systematic equity strategies (momentum, value, and a blend of the two) against a passive equal-weight benchmark on a 17-stock universe over 2021–2024. Results are reported as the backtest produced them, including where the active strategies fell short.

This page and the three below it are the first study (17 stocks, 2021 to 2026). The main result is the large-universe study of about 500 stocks from 2012.

Cause: Yahoo Finance rewrites old prices after a stock split, but SEC book value per share does not change, so price-to-book was too low for Walmart (2024) and NextEra (2020). Scope: Value figures in the charts and tables below are shown as originally published. Out-of-sample results are essentially unchanged and nothing is statistically significant in either version. Detail: repository, Amendment 8 and results/value_split_*.csv.

Headline finding: No active strategy could be distinguished from a passive benchmark holding all 17 stocks in equal weight. After the split correction (see the erratum above), Value's in-sample Sharpe ratio is 0.61, below the benchmark's 0.81. Splitting the window, Combined led in 2023–2024. None of these differences is statistically significant on 24 to 48 months of data, so they were treated as hypotheses for a pre-registered out-of-sample test. On the 20 months that followed (January 2025 to August 2026), the benchmark had the highest Sharpe ratio of the four; Value's alpha was close to zero, while Combined's alpha kept its direction, though not at a significant level. See the Strategy Analysis page for the full breakdown, significance tests and the Out of Sample tab.
