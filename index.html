Do momentum and value still work in the S&P 500?

A pre-specified test of 11-month momentum, price-to-book value and their average on about 500 large US stocks, January 2012 to August 2026.

Short answer Not detectably. None of the three signals can be shown to rank next-month returns better than chance, and the portfolios built on them do not reliably beat an equal-weight benchmark. The design was pre-specified and committed in advance, every test is counted, and the study states how small an effect it could have found.

Reading guide four terms used below
Rank IC

Each month, rank stocks by the signal and by their next-month return, then correlate the two rankings. +1 is perfect, 0 is no information, -1 is backwards. Useful signals usually score only a few hundredths.

90% interval

The range of values the data are consistent with. If it contains zero, the result cannot be told apart from no effect.

Sharpe ratio

Return above the risk-free rate per unit of volatility. A Sharpe difference compares a portfolio with its benchmark; negative means it did worse.

Group

One fifth of the stocks, ranked on the signal. Group 5 has the highest signal.

Why believe it how the study avoids fooling itself
Pre-specified

The design and every later change are dated, append-only amendments, committed before the code that depends on them.

No look-ahead, no tuning

Signals use data to the end of the previous month, book values enter on the filing date, and nothing was varied to improve a result.

Power is stated

With 108 months the test finds only a fairly large effect. A null here means "no evidence of an effect", not "proof of none".

It found its own errors

A one-month look-ahead, a split bug that flattered Value, unit errors in SEC share counts and a mislabelled Momentum signal were all caught, fixed and logged on the Data page.
