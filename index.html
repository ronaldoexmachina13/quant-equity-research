Do momentum and value still work in the S&P 500?

A pre-specified test of 11-month momentum, price-to-book value and their average on about 500 large US stocks, January 2012 to August 2026.

Registered signal tests rank IC, quintile spread and Fama-MacBeth slopes

Rank ICICt=ρS(si,t−1,ri,t)IC¯=1T∑t=1TICt

Across stocks i, the Spearman rank correlation between the signal known at the end of month t−1 and the return in month t; the test statistic is the mean over the T months, with a 90% bootstrap interval.

Quintile spreadSt=r¯5,t−r¯1,t

The equal-weighted mean return in month t of the fifth of stocks with the highest signal at t−1 (group 5) minus that of the lowest fifth (group 1), averaged over months.

Registered portfolio comparisons Sharpe difference against the equal-weight benchmark, before costs

Sharpe differenceΔSR=SRp−SRbSR=Ra−Rfσa

p is the portfolio and b its equal-weight benchmark. Ra is the annualised return, Rf the compounded Treasury bill return over the same months, and σa the annualised volatility (the monthly standard deviation times the square root of 12). The interval is a paired bootstrap of ΔSR.

Top-decile portfolios at equal weight, rebalanced monthly. Each portfolio sits beside the equal-weight benchmark of the stocks it could choose from; the Momentum benchmark starts later because the signal needs 11 months of history. SPY is shown as an outside reference and is a different universe. Sharpe ratios use the three-month Treasury bill. Turnover is the average share of the portfolio traded each month, including drift. After costs charges 10 bps on each one-way trade. The Sharpe difference and its interval are on the Portfolios page.

Relative performance top-decile portfolio against the equal-weight benchmark of the same stocks

Cumulative return of each top-decile portfolio divided by that of its own equal-weight benchmark, before costs, on a log scale. A line above 1.0 means the portfolio is ahead. Momentum needs 11 months of history, so it has its own benchmark; Value and Combined share one. Prices come from stocks that were in the index at some point and are still tradable today, which flatters every line; the Universe page measures how much. The Portfolios page has growth, drawdowns and costs.
