Portfolios

Do top-ranked stocks beat the benchmark? Secondary evidence. Each month the highest-ranked names are held at equal weight against an equal-weight benchmark of every name with usable data for that signal. Results are read next to the signal tests, not instead of them.

Headline reading

Growth of $1

Log scale, so equal slopes are equal percentage growth. The benchmark is the equal-weight average of every name with usable data for that signal, which isolates the signal from the universe. The two test periods are chained for display and reported separately in the table. Levels are inflated by survivorship, and differences are also affected, so read both with the coverage on the Universe page.

Sharpe ratios use the three-month Treasury bill as the risk-free rate. Turnover is the average share of the portfolio traded each month, including drift. After costs charges 10 bps on each one-way trade. A difference is flagged (bold, with an asterisk) only when its 90% paired bootstrap interval excludes zero.

Drawdown

Percentage below each series' previous high, from monthly data, which understates intramonth drawdowns.

Rolling 36-month excess return

Each point is the annualised geometric return of the portfolio minus that of its benchmark over the trailing 36 months. A persistent signal would sit mostly above zero. Sign changes across windows indicate period dependence, which is what an interval that includes zero reflects.

Sharpe difference against the benchmark all 18 headline comparisons, before costs; independent of the selection bar

Each bar is the paired bootstrap interval for the difference in Sharpe ratio between a portfolio and its benchmark (5,000 resamples). At the 90% level, about 1.8 of 18 intervals would exclude zero under the null. Two do: Value in 2012 to 2020, where the portfolio Sharpe ratio is below the benchmark. They are one finding, since the decile and quintile portfolios overlap, and it is absent from the re-test and the full period. Some secondary signal tests lean the same way, but they share the same months and stocks and are not independent evidence.
