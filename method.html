Method

What was done, in what order, and what the design could and could not detect. The design and every later change were recorded before the code that depends on them.

Specification what was tested
Universe
S&P 500 members, about 500 stocks, 176 months from January 2012 to August 2026
Signals
Momentum: 11-month return, no skipped month. Value: negative price-to-book. Combined: average of the two z-scores
Primary tests
Mean monthly rank IC, quintile spread, Fama-MacBeth slope, each with a 90% bootstrap interval
Portfolios
Top decile and top quintile, equal weight, monthly rebalance, against an equal-weight benchmark. Costs of 10 bps one way are shown separately
Periods
2012 to 2020 (108 months) is the new test. 2021 to 2026 (68 months) is a re-test, because those months were seen in an earlier 17-stock study
Timing
Signals use data to the end of the previous month. Book values enter on the filing date

Design in the order it was applied

  1. Question. Do simple momentum and value signals rank large US stocks by next-month return, and do portfolios built from them beat an equal-weight benchmark?
  2. Signals. Momentum is the return from 12 months before the trade date to the end of the previous month, with no month skipped. Value is the negative price-to-book ratio. Combined is the average of the two cross-sectional z-scores. Definitions were frozen from the 17-stock study. No lookback, weight or threshold was varied to improve a result. A true 12-1 version was run afterwards as a labelled sensitivity (Amendment 15) and changes no reading.
  3. Timing. A signal dated t uses only information available at the end of month t-1 and is held through month t. Book value is aligned to the date each filing became public.
  4. Primary evidence. Rank IC, quintile spread and Fama-MacBeth slopes, each with a 90% bootstrap interval (5,000 resamples, seed 42). Two test periods are reported separately, 2012 to 2020 and 2021 to 2026, plus the full sample.
  5. Secondary evidence. Top-decile and top-quintile portfolios at equal weight, rebalanced monthly, with 10 bps one-way costs charged on drift-inclusive turnover. The Sharpe difference against the benchmark is tested with a paired bootstrap using the Treasury bill rate.
  6. Reading rule. A difference counts only if its 90% interval excludes zero, and portfolio results are read only next to the signal tests. The headline holds 18 intervals, so about two would exclude zero by chance.

Limitations

  1. Survivorship. Acquired and bankrupt companies mostly have no free price history. A delisting-aware source such as CRSP is the real fix. Which way the bias runs for each strategy is not tested here and need not be the same for all of them.
  2. Free data. Wikipedia membership is approximate early on. Some book values rest on share counts that may carry unit errors the validity rule cannot detect. Ticker reuse is not fully detectable. Filings that show negative book equity are dropped, so those companies keep an older positive book value for up to six months or leave the Value and Combined tests.
  3. Universe. Large, liquid S&P 500 names are expected to be a harder place to find a factor premium, because they are closely analysed and cheap to trade. That is a hypothesis, not tested here.
  4. Signals. Price-to-book is not sector-adjusted, Momentum is not volatility-scaled, and there is no quality factor.
  5. Costs. A flat 10 bps one way, with no market impact or capacity analysis.
  6. Multiplicity. 18 headline portfolio intervals at the 90% level, so about two would exclude zero by chance.
  7. Sharpe convention. The Sharpe ratio is the annualised return minus the compounded risk-free return, divided by annualised volatility. The arithmetic version, mean monthly excess return over volatility, gives differences that are mostly 5% to 25% smaller across the 18 gross portfolio comparisons and changes no sign. It was recomputed from the saved monthly returns after the results and is not saved in a result file or part of the registered tests. Of the 18 differences, 17 are smaller (15 of them by 5% to 25%) and one is about 19% larger (Momentum top decile, 2021 to 2026).
  8. Inference. A four-factor Fama-French regression was added after the results (Amendment 18): none of the 18 portfolio-period regressions of the registered portfolios has an alpha interval that excludes zero, before costs. Newey-West and block-bootstrap intervals were added after the results (Amendment 14) and give the same result as the registered ones: none of the 33 signal tests excludes zero under any method.
Glossary terms used on every page
Registered, Exploratory, Sensitivity, Descriptive
Registered: specified before any result and counted toward the headline. Exploratory: added after the results and never counted toward the headline. Sensitivity: a registered test under a changed assumption. Descriptive: no test.
Rank IC
Each month, the Spearman rank correlation between the signal and the next month's stock returns across stocks, averaged over months.
Quintile spread
Average return of the top fifth of stocks by signal minus that of the bottom fifth.
Fama-MacBeth slope
Each month, the slope from a cross-sectional regression of next-month returns on the signal; the average slope over months.
90% bootstrap interval
A range from resampling months with replacement 5,000 times (seed 42). If it spans zero the result is read as no detectable effect.
Sharpe difference
The portfolio Sharpe ratio minus the benchmark Sharpe ratio, annualised, using the three-month Treasury bill rate as the risk-free rate.
Detectable effect
The smallest true mean rank IC the test would find with 80% probability at a 10% two-sided level, using the sample's own standard deviation. Approximate.
Survivorship
Bias that arises when index members removed during the sample are missing from the price data.
Matched-benchmark alpha
The intercept from regressing the portfolio's monthly return in excess of the Treasury bill rate on the same excess return of its equal-weight benchmark, built from the same usable names, with Newey-West 90% intervals, multiplied by 12 (Amendment 16).
Four-factor alpha
The intercept from regressing the portfolio's monthly return in excess of the Treasury bill rate on the market, size (SMB), value (HML) and momentum (Mom) factors published by Kenneth French, with Newey-West 90% intervals, multiplied by 12 (Amendment 18).
Gross, net
Gross is before transaction costs; net is after.
