Method

What was done, in what order, and what the design could and could not detect. The design and every later change were recorded before the code that depends on them.

1 · Design in the order it was applied
  1. Question. Do simple momentum and value signals rank large US stocks by next-month return, and do portfolios built from them beat an equal-weight benchmark?
  2. Signals. Momentum is the trailing 12-month return excluding the latest month. Value is the negative price-to-book ratio. Combined is the average of the two cross-sectional z-scores. Definitions were frozen from the 17-stock study. No lookback, weight or threshold was varied.
  3. Timing. A signal dated t uses data up to the end of month t-1 and is paired with the return in month t. Book value is aligned to the date each filing became public.
  4. Primary evidence. Rank IC, quintile spread and Fama-MacBeth slopes, each with a 90% bootstrap interval (5,000 resamples, seed 42). Two test periods are reported separately, 2012 to 2020 and 2021 to 2026, plus the full sample.
  5. Secondary evidence. Top-decile and top-quintile portfolios at equal weight, rebalanced monthly, with 10 bps one-way costs charged on drift-inclusive turnover. The Sharpe difference against the benchmark is tested with a paired bootstrap using the Treasury bill rate.
  6. Reading rule. A difference counts only if its 90% interval excludes zero, and portfolio results are read only next to the signal tests. The headline holds 18 intervals, so about two would exclude zero by chance.

4 · Limitations
  1. Survivorship. Acquired and bankrupt companies mostly have no free price history. A delisting-aware source such as CRSP is the real fix.
  2. Free data. Wikipedia membership is approximate early on. Some book values rest on share counts that may carry unit errors the validity rule cannot detect. Ticker reuse is not fully detectable. Filings that show negative book equity are dropped, so those companies keep an older positive book value for up to six months or leave the Value and Combined tests.
  3. Universe. Large, liquid S&P 500 names are the hardest place to find a factor premium.
  4. Signals. Price-to-book is not sector-adjusted, Momentum is not volatility-scaled, and there is no quality factor.
  5. Costs. A flat 10 bps one way, with no market impact or capacity analysis.
  6. Multiplicity. 18 headline portfolio intervals at the 90% level, so about two would exclude zero by chance.
  7. Inference. No Fama-French controls. Newey-West and block-bootstrap intervals were added after the results (Amendment 14) and agree with the registered ones: none of the 33 signal tests excludes zero under any method.
